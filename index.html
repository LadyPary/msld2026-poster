Improving Hallucination Detection in Dialogue

Parisa Rabbani, Dilek Hakkani-Tür
Department of Computer Science, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign
MSLD 2026
Paper Poster MSLD 2026

* The poster contains more recent results than the writeup.

Overview

We conducted two lines of mitigation experiments on DiaHalu across models.

Dialogue Transformations (Dataset: DiaHalu, Transformer LLM: GPT-4o mini, Detector LLMs: Claude-Haiku-4.5, GPT-5.4-nano, GPT-5.4-mini, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-3-4B, Gemma-3-12B, Gemma-3-27B): We applied surface-level and content-level transformations to dialogues before detection, testing whether restructuring the input (e.g., flattening, summarization, turn concatenation, dehumanization) improves or degrades hallucination detection.

Dialog Native Approaches (Dataset: DiaHalu, Detector LLMs: GPT-5.4-mini, GPT-5.4-nano, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B, Gemma-3-27B): We evaluated detection strategies that operate over the dialogue structure itself, including inline checkpoints, checkpoints with history repetition, and progressive exchange-level detection, and compared them against standard zero-shot detection and their OR ensembles.

Dialogue Transformations

Base dialogue:

A: Have you heard this slogan? I can't remember what drinking Red Bull gives you.

B: It can give you wings!

Surface Transforms

S1 Coreference resolution

A: Have you heard this slogan? I can't remember what drinking Red Bull gives you. B: Drinking Red Bull can give you wings!

Win: 0, Lose: 0, NS: 7
S2 Formalization

A: Have you heard this slogan? I cannot recall what drinking Red Bull provides.

B: It can provide you with wings.

Win: 0, Lose: 3, NS: 4
S3 Turn concatenation

Have you heard this slogan? I can't remember what drinking Red Bull gives you. It can give you wings!

Win: 2, Lose: 0, NS: 5
S4 Dehumanization

AI Agent 1: Have you heard this slogan? I can't remember what drinking Red Bull gives you. AI Agent 2: It can give you wings!

Win: 2, Lose: 0, NS: 5

Content Transforms

F1 Flattening to prose

Person A cannot remember what drinking Red Bull gives you. In regard to the slogan, it can give you wings.

Win: 1, Lose: 2, NS: 4
F2 Atomic claim decomposition

There is a slogan associated with drinking Red Bull.
The speaker cannot remember what drinking Red Bull gives you.
Drinking Red Bull can give you wings.

Win: 1, Lose: 3, NS: 3
F3 Dialogue summarization

A1 mentioned not being able to remember the slogan related to drinking Red Bull. B1 responded that drinking Red Bull can give you wings.

Win: 0, Lose: 2, NS: 5
F4 QA conversion

Q: What does drinking Red Bull give you? A: Drinking Red Bull can give you wings.

Win: 1, Lose: 4, NS: 2

We evaluated eight transformations (S1, S2, S3, S4, F1, F2, F3, F4) plus the base condition across all seven detector LLMs on 1,103 dialogues (~69,500 evaluations). Content transforms that remove dialogue structure consistently degraded detection: QA conversion (F4) showed the largest losses (Win: 1, Lose: 4, NS: 2), followed by atomic claim decomposition (F2; Win: 1, Lose: 3, NS: 3) and formalization (S2; Win: 0, Lose: 3, NS: 4). Flattening to prose (F1) and dialogue summarization (F3) also hurt performance. Coreference resolution (S1) was neutral (Win: 0, Lose: 0, NS: 7).

Structure-preserving transforms fared better: turn concatenation (S3) and dehumanization (S4) both showed gains (Win: 2, Lose: 0, NS: 5). One possible explanation for the limited overall effectiveness of the transformations is dataset staleness: DiaHalu was released in 2024, and current models may have encountered it during training, reducing its sensitivity as an evaluation benchmark. We plan to test these methods on our new dynamically generated benchmark to determine whether the transforms show stronger effects on unseen data.

OR ensemble consistently outperforms both individual approaches

OR-ensembling Zero-Shot (ZS) with Checkpoints+Repetition (Ckp+Rep) yielded statistically significant F1 improvements over ZS alone on all five models (95% bootstrap CI), with gains ranging from +2.4 (GPT-5.4-nano) to +6.2 (Qwen3-8B). Inline Checkpoints (Ckp), Progressive Detection (Exch), and Ckp+Rep alone did not beat ZS on their own, yet the OR ensemble recovers the strengths of both.

ZS and Ckp+Rep are complementary, not redundant

Per-hallucination-type recall on GPT-5.4-mini (n=1,103) reveals that ZS dominates on reasoning errors (0.937 vs. 0.732 recall), while Ckp+Rep outperforms ZS on four of five hallucination types, with the largest margins on overreliance (0.767 vs. 0.533, +23 recall) and irrelevance (0.397 vs. 0.302, +10 recall). The disagreement is near-symmetric: 64 hallucinations were caught only by Ckp+Rep and 62 only by ZS, confirming that single-pass detection has blind spots on dialogue-specific errors that exchange-level auditing catches.

Domain-level analysis confirms complementary strengths

Per-domain F1 on GPT-5.4-mini (n=1,103) shows the OR ensemble wins three of four domains: Chit-Chat (0.595 vs. ZS 0.544), Task-oriented (0.570 vs. ZS 0.446), and World Knowledge (0.737 vs. ZS 0.669). ZS retains the lead only on Reasoning (0.924 vs. OR 0.910), where its strong single-pass reasoning recall carries through. Ckp+Rep shows its largest individual advantage over ZS on Task-oriented (0.547 vs. 0.446), the domain most dependent on tracking multi-turn task state.

Results

OR-ensembling Zero-Shot with Checkpoints+Repetition yields significant F1 gains on all 5 models (+3 to +6 F1), while neither approach wins alone.

Ckp+Rep beats ZS on 4 of 5 hallucination types; ZS dominates reasoning errors. The ensemble recovers both.

OR ensemble wins 3 of 4 domains; ZS retains the lead on Reasoning.
