Improving Hallucination Detection in Dialogue

Parisa Rabbani, Dilek Hakkani-Tür
Department of Computer Science, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign
MSLD 2026
Paper Poster MSLD 2026

* The poster contains more recent results than the writeup.

Overview

LLM-based hallucination detectors are typically evaluated on sentence- or passage-level benchmarks, leaving their behavior on multi-turn dialogue largely unexplored. We study how dialogue structure affects detection accuracy, finding that models invent speaker names, fabricate details, and lose track of earlier turns as conversations grow longer.

We propose a suite of dialogue transformations and dialogue-native detection approaches evaluated on the DiaHalu benchmark across seven detector LLMs (Claude-Haiku-4.5, GPT-5.4-nano, GPT-5.4-mini, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-3-4B, Gemma-3-12B, Gemma-3-27B). Our analysis reveals that transforms preserving dialogue structure improve detection, while those that flatten or remove it hurt performance.

Key Findings

Dialogue structure matters

Transforms that simplify or remove dialogue structure (F1–F4, S2) hurt detection; transforms that preserve it (S3, S4, S7) help.

Single-pass detection has blind spots

Zero-shot detection misses dialogue-specific errors (overreliance, irrelevance) that exchange-level auditing catches.

Complementarity beats sophistication

The strongest detector is not a new method, but the OR ensemble of the simplest approach (ZS) and a dialogue-native auditor (Ckp+Rep).

Hallucination in Dialogue

Factuality

A: How tall is Mount Everest?

B: About 5,000 feet.

A: Shorter than I thought!

B: Yes, it surprises many people!

Faithfulness

A: I'm a vegan, I believe in treating all creatures with kindness.

B: That sounds delicious! I'm always up for good food.

A: Shall we go get some beef tacos?

B: That's great! I'm vegan too.

Results

OR-ensembling Zero-Shot with Checkpoints+Repetition yields significant F1 gains on all 5 models (+3 to +6 F1), while neither approach wins alone.

Ckp+Rep beats ZS on 4 of 5 hallucination types; ZS dominates reasoning errors. The ensemble recovers both.

OR ensemble wins 3 of 4 domains; ZS retains the lead on Reasoning.

Complementary error profiles

ZS dominates reasoning errors (+21 recall), Ckp+Rep wins on dialogue-specific errors like overreliance (+23 recall) and irrelevance (+10 recall); the ensemble recovers both.

Near-symmetric disagreement

64 hallucinations caught only by Ckp+Rep; 62 only by ZS. This confirms genuine complementarity rather than one method strictly dominating.
